## Primary-source arithmetic and scientific meaning

1. [Platt & Trudgian, *Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society* 53 (2021), 792–797](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12460), published 2021: rigorous interval-arithmetic work proves **all** nontrivial zeros with **0 < Im(ρ) ≤ 3 × 10¹²** lie on σ=½ and are simple. This is a bound on **imaginary height**, not 3 trillion zeros counted; RH at unrestricted heights is not settled. The app does not independently implement their verification/certificates. The symmetric negative heights are not a separate counted result.
2. [NIST DLMF §25.10](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.10) distinguishes zeros on the line, number of all zeros, the Riemann–Siegel remainder and the 1986 first ten billion zeros milestone (**count**, not **height**). The main-term lab explicitly states endpoint convention 0<Im(ρ)<T for N(T), compared with the ≤ finite-height statement. At a zero ordinate, conventions matter.
3. [Riemann–von Mangoldt asymptotic](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Riemann-vonMangoldtFormula.html): `M(T) = T/(2π)(ln(T/(2π))−1)`; `N(T) = M(T)+O(ln T)` at large T. The plotted quantity M(T) is **not** exact integer N(T), the omitted remainder is not certified zero, all natural logarithms; no sampled zeta values are plotted. Two bars have **separate logarithmic scales**. The exhibit restricts height T to [100,3×10¹²], with independently guarded JS functions, and makes no asserted exact counts at endpoints.
4. [Andrew Odlyzko’s original first-100-zeros table](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/zeta_tables/zeros2) is the primary dataset for three **truncated** illustrative first ordinates. Their display precision has not been independently recomputed or certified by this site. Table provenance is not a new certificate.
5. Distinguishes locating approximate roots from rigorously enclosing signs/interval errors and **separately** proving completeness of the count (for example with argument-principle or Turing-style methods). No displayed sample or graphic claims to be a proof of RH.
