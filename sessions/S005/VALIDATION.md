## Primary sources, exact domains and interpretation
1. Inspected [NIST DLMF §25.10(i)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.10) (2026 edition): ζ(s)≠0 for Re(s)>1 by Euler product; also Re(s)=1 wherever ζ is defined, historically Hadamard and de la Vallée Poussin (1896). At s=1, t=0, ζ has a simple pole, not a zero; −2n (positive integer n) are trivial zeros. Elsewhere Re(s)≤0 has no zeros. Nontrivial zeros are confined to 0<Re(s)<1, symmetric about Re(s)=½; RH is open.
2. Read primary peer-reviewed [Mossinghoff–Trudgian–Yang, *Research in Number Theory* 10, 11 (2024)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40993-023-00498-y), published 12 January 2024, whose abstract and theorem statements give **no zeros** when σ≥1−1/(5.558691 ln|t|) for |t|≥2 and σ≥1−1/[55.241(ln|t|)^(2/3)(ln ln|t|)^(1/3)] for |t|≥3. Explicit constants, natural logarithms, non-strict σ≥ and both signs |t| are retained. These are unconditional explicit bounds; no unverified modern-record claim. For |t|≥10, both formulas are valid and the app computes boundaries from log₁₀|t| without overflow even at 10^5000.
3. The chart maps σ positions linearly on ½≤σ≤1.05. Its shaded segment means *proved nonvanishing for σ at or to the right of the formula boundary for the selected |t|*, not that dots at other σ are known zeros. The separate plane diagram places exactly −4 and −2 trivial zeros and the pole at 1; others are outside the frame. No zeta numerical evaluation, no finite zero list, no certification. Courtyard bounds are about *counts*, not zero absence.
